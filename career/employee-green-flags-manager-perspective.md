## Big Picture

### Main Themes & Recurring Patterns
* **No Surprises Policy:** Managers consistently value employees who communicate both wins and bad news early. High performers flag emerging risks before they escalate into crises.
* **Solution-Oriented Problem Solving:** Top employees don't just dump problems onto their manager's lap; they troubleshoot small issues independently or present proposed solutions alongside the problem.
* **Workload Honesty & Transparent Prioritization:** Rather than quietly drowning or pretending capacity is fine, strong performers proactively communicate when overwhelmed and ask to collaborate on reprioritizing or delegating.
* **Autonomous Execution & Transparency:** High performers execute daily work without needing micromanagement, while maintaining enough transparency that managers can check progress without interrupting them.
* **Documentation & Risk Mitigation:** In client-facing or complex operations, high performers spend a few extra minutes documenting conversations, creating paper trails, and highlighting operational risks early.

### Disagreements & Nuances
* **Punctuality vs. Autonomy:** Some managers view strict punctuality and adherence to shift schedules as a primary green flag for reliability. Others noted that in their industries, their best performers are rarely the most punctual, valuing output, problem-solving, and autonomy far above clock-watching.

---

## What I Want to Remember
1. **Managing Up Means Zero Surprises:** Always inform leadership about incoming bad news or operational risks before they hear it from third parties or clients.
2. **Bring Solutions, Not Just Issues:** When escalating an issue, present at least one viable fix or outline the troubleshooting steps already attempted.
3. **Be Vocal About Bandwidth Limits:** Asking "I have a lot on my plate; can we work together to prioritize these tasks?" is seen as a strong green flag, not a sign of weakness.
