# Why Indian Box Office Reports Use "Mysore" Instead of "Karnataka"

When tracking Indian cinema box office collections, trade analysts and distributors do not use official state borders. Instead, they rely on traditional **theatrical distribution circuits** established decades ago. 

### Why territories like "Mysore" still exist:
* **Historical Legacy:** When the distribution network was first organized, Karnataka was known as the Princely State of Mysore. The trade simply kept the original name even after the state was officially renamed Karnataka in 1973.
* **Overlapping Borders:** Circuits are drawn based on physical film distribution logistics (and now digital keys/QUBE delivery) rather than political lines. A circuit might overflow into a neighboring state or leave certain districts out.
* **Trade Standardization:** The entire Indian film industry (producers, distributors, and trade analysts) uses these legacy data sets to keep historical comparisons uniform.

---

## The Core Indian Theatrical Circuits

The Indian box office map is generally divided into 11 to 14 core distribution territories:

### 1. The "Big Three" Hindi Circuits
These areas account for the vast majority of a Hindi film's domestic revenue:
* **Mumbai (Bombay) Circuit:** Covers Mumbai, Maharashtra (excluding the Vidarbha region), Gujarat, Goa, and parts of coastal Karnataka. It is historically the highest revenue-generating territory.
* **Delhi-U.P. Circuit:** Covers Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, and Uttarakhand.
* **East Punjab Circuit:** Covers Punjab, Chandigarh, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, and Jammu & Kashmir.

### 2. The Central India Territories
This belt still utilizes administrative terminology dating back to British-era regions:
* **C.P. Berar (Central Provinces and Berar):** Covers the Vidarbha region of Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, and southern/eastern Madhya Pradesh.
* **Central India (C.I.):** Covers northern and western Madhya Pradesh.
* **Rajasthan:** Covers the entire state of Rajasthan.

### 3. The Eastern Circuits
Often grouped together by the trade, this territory uniquely crosses international borders for Hindi film distribution rights:
* **West Bengal** (including the Andaman & Nicobar Islands)
* **Bihar & Jharkhand**
* **Odisha**
* **Assam & the North-Eastern States**
* **International Additions:** Historically, distribution rights for **Nepal and Bhutan** are managed through India's Eastern Circuit.

### 4. The Southern Circuits
Because the southern states have massive local film industries (Tollywood, Kollywood, Mollywood, and Sandalwood), Bollywood tracks them as independent territorial pockets:
* **Mysore:** Covers Bengaluru and the majority of Karnataka.
* **Nizam:** Covers Telangana, the Marathwada region of Maharashtra, and parts of Karnataka (mapping out the old princely state borders ruled by the Nizam of Hyderabad).
* **Andhra:** Covers the state territory of Andhra Pradesh.
* **Tamil Nadu & Kerala:** Often tracked individually or grouped together for standalone Hindi releases.
